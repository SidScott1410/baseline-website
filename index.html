I · THE LANDSCAPE

AI's economic footprint is measured today in fragments.

Each of these efforts is valuable. None can be compared with the others, and none links usage to wages, hiring or firm output.

PUBLISHED MEASURES TODAY

  • Anthropic Economic Index
  • OpenAI, How People Use ChatGPT (NBER, 2025)
  • Microsoft AI Economy Institute diffusion reports
  • Google ATLAS usage map
  • Stanford Digital Economy Lab payroll studies

WHAT NONE OF THEM PROVIDES

  1. 1A common task taxonomy across providers
  2. 2Comparable measures of depth of use and output
  3. 3A link from AI use to wages, hiring and firm output

Baseline is built to connect them.

II · WHY NOW

The largest economic shift in a generation is being measured with assumptions.

~0.7%

added productivity over ten years (Acemoglu, 2024)

7%

added global GDP (Goldman Sachs, 2023)

Forecasts of AI's contribution to the economy disagree by an order of magnitude. The labor-market evidence is mostly nulls and mixed results.

Why comparable data matters

The disagreement is not a failure of economics. It is a failure of data. No company can publish a comparable index with its competitors, and public institutions cannot see provider data. So investment, hiring and policy decisions rest on assumed parameters.

Baseline replaces assumed parameters with measured ones.

Four parameters: task share, adoption, productivity, and automation versus augmentation.

How the scenario tracker works

Each scenario rests on a handful of measurable parameters: the share of tasks AI performs, how widely it is adopted, the productivity gain per task, and how much work is automated rather than augmented. Today, none of the three can be ruled out. Baseline measures those parameters across providers and publishes, every quarter, where the evidence points.

Scenarios from Korinek, Jones, Sacher, Cotter and McCrory, "Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI," The Anthropic Institute Working Paper 2026-02. Explore the scenarios

Your data stays yours.

Raw data never leaves its owner. Each provider runs agreed queries on its own systems, and only aggregates are shared. Analysis plans are registered before any data is opened, and an independent steering committee sets methodology and decides releases.

Baseline is forming its founding group of AI providers, data platforms and economists.

IV · WORKING GROUPS

Six working groups build the measurement stack, from task to world model.

Each group pairs provider engineers with independent economists and publishes its specifications openly. Together they produce the measured parameters an AI economy world model needs.

PHASE 1

Measure

WG 1

Task ontology

A versioned crosswalk from provider logs to O*NET tasks, extended to multi-step and agentic work.

Delivers the Baseline Task Taxonomy and an open classifier.

WG 2

Output and verification

Quality-adjusted output: cost per verified task, depth of use, and a price deflator for AI-produced work.

Delivers the output-unit specification and verification protocols.

WG 3

Privacy-preserving aggregation

Federated queries, minimum cell sizes and differential privacy, so only safe aggregates leave each provider.

Delivers the query protocol and disclosure-review rules.

PHASE 2

Estimate

WG 4

Causal identification

Linking AI use to wages, hiring and firm output, using staggered rollouts, price changes and outages as natural experiments.

Delivers pre-registered study designs and linked research panels.

WG 5

Compute production functions

How compute converts to output by sector: elasticities, substitution between labor and compute, and energy and capacity limits.

Delivers sector-level elasticity estimates with stated uncertainty.

PHASE 3

Simulate

WG 6

The AI economy world model

How the world model is built

An open, calibrated simulation of the AI economy that combines agent-based and general-equilibrium models and runs across adoption regimes, from augmentation to autonomous agents. Every parameter traces to an estimate from Phases 1 and 2.

Delivers a quarterly scenario tracker from 2027, then the open model and a public scenario library in 2028.

Governance and independence sit with the steering committee, across all six groups.

V · QUESTIONS

What partners and policymakers ask first.

Is sharing usage data between competitors an antitrust risk?+

Only aggregates leave each provider, through a neutral host, under clean-team rules. No pricing, capacity or customer-level data is exchanged, and antitrust counsel reviews the protocol before Phase 1 begins.

How is Baseline different from existing usage indices?+

The Anthropic Economic Index, OpenAI's ChatGPT study, Microsoft's diffusion reports and Google's ATLAS each describe one provider. Payroll studies link AI exposure to jobs, but not to measured usage. Baseline makes usage comparable across providers and links it to outcomes.

Can linked data identify causal effects?+

Phase 2 relies on variation firms do not choose: staggered rollouts, price and rate-limit changes, outages and model releases. Analysis plans are registered before any data opens.

How is privacy protected?+

Raw data never leaves its owner. Providers run agreed queries on their own systems, and results are released only above minimum cell sizes, with differential-privacy protections and disclosure review.
